How much does an AI chatbot cost for a small business?

Subscription tools, custom builds, usage fees and setup — what small businesses really pay for an AI chatbot in 2026, and how to work out if it's worth it.

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Book a free strategy call → Get a free website audit

Short answer: most small businesses pay roughly $20–$150 per month for an off-the-shelf AI chatbot tool, while a done-for-you custom assistant typically costs a one-time setup fee in the low thousands plus modest monthly running costs. Several 2026 pricing guides, including Denser's chatbot pricing guide, report similar ranges.

The three ways to get an AI chatbot

ApproachTypical costTrade-off
DIY subscription tool~$20–$150/monthCheap and quick, but you configure, train and maintain it yourself
Done-for-you setup on a platformSetup fee + subscriptionProfessionally trained and connected to your calendar/CRM
Fully custom assistantLow thousands up front + usageMost control over behaviour, data and integrations

What affects the price

  • Conversation volume — many tools charge per message or per resolved conversation.
  • Channels — website only, or also WhatsApp, Instagram, SMS and email.
  • Integrations — booking calendars, CRMs, e-commerce and helpdesk tools.
  • Training and testing — writing the knowledge base, guardrails and test scenarios.
  • Human handover — live chat seats or escalation workflows.

Does a chatbot pay for itself?

Use simple maths. If your average new client is worth $1,000 and the assistant books just one extra client a month from after-hours or slow-reply enquiries, a $100/month tool or even a few thousand in setup pays back quickly. The bigger win is speed: businesses that respond within minutes reach far more leads than those that reply hours later — see our speed-to-lead guide.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. What happens when the bot doesn't know the answer?
  2. Can it book directly into my calendar?
  3. Where is conversation data stored, and is it GDPR-compliant for EU/UK customers?
  4. Who updates it when my prices or services change?
  5. How will I see which conversations turned into clients?

FAQ

Common questions.

Are free AI chatbots good enough?

Free tiers are fine for testing, but they usually limit conversations, branding and integrations. Most businesses that rely on a chatbot for leads end up on a paid plan.

Can an AI chatbot replace a receptionist?

It can handle routine questions and bookings around the clock, freeing your team for complex conversations. Most businesses use it alongside people, not instead of them.
