Zapier and Make vs custom automation: when should you switch?

No-code automation tools are one of the best things to happen to small businesses. But they have limits. Here's how to tell when a workflow has outgrown them, and what a custom build adds.

By the WebersLink team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Start with no-code. Seriously.

Tools like Zapier and Make let you connect apps and automate simple workflows without writing code: "when a form is submitted, add a row to a sheet and send me a message". For many businesses, that is all they ever need. They're quick to set up, easy to change and affordable at low volumes. If you're just starting with automation, try them first.

Signs a workflow has outgrown no-code tools

  • The bill keeps climbing. Most no-code tools charge by tasks or operations. A workflow with many steps that runs thousands of times a month can become expensive.
  • The logic is a maze. Lots of branches, filters and paths become hard to understand, and nobody wants to touch it in case it breaks.
  • Failures go unnoticed. A step fails silently, and you find out when a customer complains.
  • You need things the tool can't do. Complex data handling, waiting for replies, retrying safely, or talking to a system with no ready-made connector.
  • It's business-critical. If it stops, you lose money, and it needs proper testing, monitoring and version control.
  • Data rules matter. You need to control exactly where customer data goes and how long it's kept.

What custom automation adds

A custom automation is code written for your workflow, running on a server or cloud function. Compared with no-code tools, it can offer:

  • Predictable cost: hosting is usually a flat, modest amount, whether it runs a hundred or a hundred thousand times.
  • Any logic you need, including waiting, retries, scheduling and complex decisions.
  • Reliability: proper error handling, alerts and protection against doing the same thing twice.
  • Testing: automated tests that check every change before it goes live.
  • Ownership: the code is yours and isn't tied to one vendor's pricing or features.

The trade-offs are a higher up-front cost and the need for someone who can maintain the code.

The hybrid approach most businesses end up with

You don't have to choose one for everything. A sensible setup keeps simple, low-volume workflows in no-code tools, where anyone can tweak them, and moves the heavy, critical or complex workflows into custom code. Over time, you rebuild whatever becomes expensive or fragile.

A real example

Cold email outreach is a good example of something that outgrows no-code tools. It needs several mailboxes sending at a natural pace, warm-up limits, threaded follow-ups, reading replies and bounces, and stopping instantly when someone answers, all without ever emailing someone twice after a crash. We built this as a custom system, our Outreach Engine, because stitching it together from no-code steps would have been costly and fragile.

How to decide for each workflow

  1. Is it simple and low-volume? Use a no-code tool.
  2. Is it costing a lot each month in tasks or operations? Price a custom version.
  3. Would a silent failure cost you money or customers? Build it properly, with monitoring.
  4. Does it need logic your tool struggles with? Custom code will be simpler in the long run.

FAQ

Common questions.

Is Zapier or Make better?

Both are capable. Zapier is often easier for beginners and has a huge app library; Make offers more visual control over complex scenarios. The right choice depends on your apps and how complex your workflows are.

Can you move my existing Zaps to custom code?

Yes. We usually rebuild one workflow at a time, run it alongside the old version to check the results match, then switch over.

Who maintains custom automations?

Whoever builds them should document and monitor them. Many clients have us look after their automations as part of an in-house team plan.
