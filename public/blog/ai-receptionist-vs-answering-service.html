AI receptionist vs answering service: which should you choose?

Both answer the calls you can't. One is software, one is people. Here is how they compare on cost, quality and what they can actually do, so you can pick the right one for your business.

By the WebersLink team · Updated November 2026 · 7 min read

Why this decision matters

Most callers who reach voicemail don't leave a message. They try the next business. Whether you choose a human answering service or an AI receptionist, the goal is the same: make sure every caller reaches someone who can help, at any hour. The right choice depends on the kind of calls you get.

What a human answering service does

An answering service is a team of trained receptionists who answer your calls using a script you agree with them. They typically take messages, pass on urgent calls and, with some services, book appointments in a shared calendar. You usually pay per minute, per call or a monthly plan with a set allowance.

Strengths: human empathy, good with upset or confused callers, and comfortable with unusual situations. Weaknesses: costs rise with every call, quality varies between agents, and receptionists handling many businesses may only know what's on their script.

What an AI receptionist does

An AI receptionist is a voice agent that answers in a natural voice, understands what the caller wants and responds using your own information: services, prices, areas, opening hours and policies. Connected to your calendar, it can check real availability and book there and then. It can also text a confirmation, log the call in your CRM and send you a summary.

Strengths: answers instantly, any number of calls at once, 24/7, with the same accurate answer every time, at a much lower cost per call. Weaknesses: it needs good setup and guardrails, and some callers prefer a person for sensitive or complicated matters.

When an answering service is the better choice

  • Most of your calls are sensitive, such as legal, medical emergencies or complaints.
  • Callers often need judgement that's hard to write down as rules.
  • Your call volume is low and steady, so per-call costs stay small.

When an AI receptionist is the better choice

  • Many calls are routine: prices, availability, service areas, opening hours, booking or moving an appointment.
  • You get bursts of calls at the same time, or a lot after hours.
  • You want bookings to land straight in your calendar without anyone copying details across.
  • You want a written record of every call.

The best of both: a hybrid setup

Many businesses get the best results with a mix. The AI receptionist answers every call first, handles the routine ones and books appointments, then transfers anything urgent, sensitive or unusual to a person: you, your team, or a human answering service. Callers are never left waiting, your people only deal with calls that need them, and costs stay predictable.

How to decide in 15 minutes

  1. List your last 30 calls and mark each one routine or complex.
  2. Note how many came in after hours or while you were busy.
  3. If most calls are routine, start with an AI receptionist and a clear handover rule.
  4. If most are complex, start with people, and consider AI for after-hours only.

FAQ

Common questions.

Will callers be annoyed talking to an AI?

Most callers care about getting a quick, accurate answer. A well-built agent introduces itself as an AI assistant, keeps calls short and hands over to a person whenever the caller asks or the call needs it.

Can an AI receptionist transfer calls to me?

Yes. You decide the rules, for example transferring urgent jobs to your mobile and taking messages for everything else.

Can I use an AI receptionist only after hours?

Yes. Many businesses forward calls to the AI agent only outside opening hours or when nobody picks up within a few rings.
