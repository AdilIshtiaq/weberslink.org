Why is my website not getting leads? 9 common fixes

Visitors are arriving but the enquiries aren't. These are the nine problems we find most often when we audit small business websites — and how to fix each one.

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Book a walkthrough Get a free website audit

1. Visitors can't tell what you do in 5 seconds

If your headline is "Welcome to our website" or a vague slogan, people leave. Say who you help, what you do and where: "Bookkeeping for restaurants in London".

2. There's no clear next step

Pick one primary action — book a call, get a quote — and repeat it in the header, after every section and at the bottom. Too many choices means no choice.

3. Your forms ask too much

Every extra field loses people. Ask only what you need to respond: name, email or phone, and one or two qualifying questions.

4. It's slow, especially on mobile

Most visitors are on phones, often on mobile data. Heavy images, page builders and plugins slow things down. Test with Google PageSpeed Insights and fix the biggest issues first.

5. There's no proof

Reviews, real project photos, client logos, case studies and credentials do more selling than any paragraph about "quality and service".

6. Prices are a total mystery

You don't need a full price list, but "projects typically start from $X" filters out bad-fit leads and gives good ones the confidence to contact you.

7. You're attracting the wrong traffic

Ranking for broad informational terms brings visitors who aren't ready to buy. Make sure your key pages target searches with buying intent — e.g. "kitchen remodeler near me" rather than "kitchen ideas".

8. Enquiries arrive but nobody replies fast

Sometimes the leads are there — they just go cold. Responding within minutes rather than hours makes a big difference (see speed to lead). An AI assistant or automated follow-up fixes this.

9. You're not tracking anything

Without tracking form submissions, calls and bookings, you can't tell which pages and channels work. Set up privacy-friendly analytics and conversion events.

FAQ

Common questions.

What is a good conversion rate for a small business website?

It varies by industry and traffic source, but many service business sites convert somewhere around 2–5% of visitors into enquiries. If you're well below that, the fixes above usually help.

Should I redesign or fix my current website?

If the problems are copy, calls-to-action and forms, fix them first. If the site is slow, dated and hard to edit, a redesign is usually better value.
