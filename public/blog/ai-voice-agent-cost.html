How much does an AI voice agent cost?

AI voice agents can answer your phone 24/7, answer questions and book appointments. Here is what goes into the price, the typical ranges you'll see, and a simple way to check whether one pays for itself.

By the WebersLink team · Updated November 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer

An AI voice agent has two kinds of cost: a one-time setup to design, train and connect it, and a running cost that mostly depends on how many minutes it spends on the phone. For a small business, setup usually ranges from a few hundred dollars for a simple do-it-yourself agent to several thousand for a custom agent connected to your calendar, CRM and phone system. Running costs are often billed per minute of conversation, so a business that takes a few hundred short calls a month pays far less than a busy clinic or contact centre.

The rest of this guide breaks those numbers down so you can estimate your own cost before you speak to anyone.

What you are paying for

Every AI voice agent, however it is sold, is built from the same parts:

  • Speech recognition, which turns the caller's voice into text in real time.
  • A language model, which understands the request and decides what to say or do next.
  • A synthetic voice, which turns the reply back into natural-sounding speech.
  • Telephony: a phone number and the connection that carries the call.
  • Integrations: your calendar, CRM, booking system, email or SMS, so the agent can actually book, look up or record things.
  • Knowledge and rules: your services, prices, opening hours, FAQs and the guardrails that decide what the agent may say and when it hands over to a person.

Platforms bundle the first four into a per-minute price. The last two are where most of the setup work, and most of the quality, comes from.

Running costs: per minute, per call or per month

Most voice AI platforms charge per minute of conversation. Depending on the provider, the voice quality and the language model, that is often somewhere between a few cents and a few tens of cents per minute, plus the cost of the phone number and call minutes from your telephony provider. Some services instead sell a monthly plan with a set number of minutes or calls included.

To estimate your own running cost, multiply three numbers:

  1. Calls per month the agent will answer (all calls, or only after-hours and overflow).
  2. Average call length in minutes. Booking and FAQ calls are often two to four minutes.
  3. The all-in price per minute from the platform you choose.

For example, 300 calls a month at three minutes each is 900 minutes. At an illustrative 15 cents a minute, that is about $135 a month in usage. Always check current pricing, because providers change it often.

Setup costs: what changes the price

Setup is where quotes differ most. These are the things that move the price up or down:

  • How much the agent needs to know. A single-service business with ten FAQs is quick to train. A clinic with dozens of treatments, practitioners and rules takes longer.
  • What it needs to do. Answering questions is simpler than checking real availability, booking, rescheduling and taking deposits.
  • Integrations. Connecting to Google Calendar or Outlook is routine. A custom practice-management system or an older phone system takes more work.
  • Call routing. Transferring urgent calls to a mobile number, sending texts and logging every call to your CRM all add value and setup time.
  • Testing. A good agent is tested against realistic calls, including awkward ones, before it goes live.
  • Languages and voices. Each extra language needs its own testing.

Ongoing tuning: the cost most people forget

An AI agent is not "set and forget". Prices change, services change, and real callers ask questions nobody predicted. The best results come from reviewing conversations regularly, fixing answers that weren't quite right and updating the knowledge base. Some providers include this in a monthly fee; with self-service tools, it is your time. Either way, budget for it.

Does it pay for itself?

The question that matters is not "what does it cost?" but "what does a missed call cost me?". Work it out like this:

  1. Estimate how many calls you miss each month: after hours, at lunch, when you're with a customer or on a job.
  2. Estimate what share of those callers would have booked if someone had answered.
  3. Multiply by the value of a typical booking or job.

If you miss 40 calls a month, a quarter of them would have booked, and an average job is worth $300, that is around $3,000 of work a month going to someone else. Even a fully custom agent is usually a small fraction of that. If you rarely miss calls, a voice agent is a convenience rather than an investment, and a chat agent on your website may be the better first step.

Do-it-yourself tools vs a custom build

Self-service platforms let you create an agent with a web dashboard. They are cheaper to start and fine for simple use cases, but you do the training, testing and integration work, and the results depend on the time you put in.

A custom-built agent costs more up front, but someone experienced designs the conversation, connects your real systems, writes the guardrails and keeps improving it. For businesses where every call is valuable, the difference in booked appointments usually outweighs the difference in price.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What exactly is included in the per-minute price, and what costs extra (phone numbers, SMS, transfers)?
  • Who trains the agent, and who updates it when my prices or services change?
  • Can it book directly into my calendar, and what happens if two people try to book the same slot?
  • How does it hand over to a person, and do I get a summary of every call?
  • Where are call recordings and transcripts stored, and for how long?
  • Can I keep my existing phone number?

FAQ

Common questions.

Is an AI voice agent cheaper than a receptionist?

For most small businesses, yes, especially for after-hours and overflow calls. A voice agent costs a setup fee plus usage, while a full-time receptionist is a salary. Many businesses use both: people during busy hours and the AI agent for everything else.

Are there hidden costs?

Watch for phone number and call charges from your telephony provider, SMS fees, transfers to a mobile number, and the time needed to keep the agent's knowledge up to date.

How long does it take to set up?

A simple agent can be live in days. A custom agent connected to your calendar and CRM usually takes one to two weeks, including testing.
